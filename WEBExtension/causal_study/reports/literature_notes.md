# Bounded primary-source novelty check

Checked online 15 October 2026. This is a targeted overlap check, not an exhaustive systematic review. The summaries describe the authors' claims; they do not independently reproduce their experiments. The following primary pages and relevant HTML sections were inspected.

1. Xu Shen et al. (EMNLP 2025), [Understanding the Information Propagation Effects of Communication Topologies in LLM-based Multi-Agent Systems](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.623/). The paper already presents causal analysis of beneficial and erroneous information propagation across sparse and dense agent topologies. Thus this study should not claim the first causal analysis of LLM communication or a new general discovery that erroneous and useful information propagate differently.

2. Yuante Li et al. (arXiv v1, 2 July 2026), [Diverse Evidence, Better Forecasts: Multi-Agent Deliberation Under Information Asymmetry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01661). [Sections 3 and 5.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.01661v1) explicitly partition evidence into shared and disjoint private subsets and study rationale sharing and aggregation. Information asymmetry as a condition for deliberation gains therefore has direct prior art. Its forecasting task differs from the proposed HotpotQA study; task difference alone does not establish substantive novelty.

3. Zihao Zhao et al. (arXiv v1, 3 October 2026), [When Debate Helps: Proposal Supply and Verification-Aware Readout in Multi-Agent Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04686). [Sections 2 and 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.04686v1) distinguish correct-proposal availability from final selection, define recoverable headroom, and hold proposal states fixed while altering peer evidence. This directly overlaps the conceptual motivation of E8 and part of E7. E8 remains valuable as an audit, but its proposal-versus-readout decomposition cannot be presented as newly invented here.

4. Yingxuan Yang et al. (arXiv v1, 3 February 2026), [Understanding Agent Scaling in LLM-Based Multi-Agent Systems via Diversity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03794). This work links complementary evidence and correlated channels to heterogeneous agent scaling. It is relevant background for the information-access hypothesis, rather than evidence that this particular placement intervention has already been evaluated.

A potentially narrower contribution is a crossed, same-document-union placement intervention with fixed source delivery, matched receiver revision calls, transparent message provenance, and separate proposal/aggregation diagnostics. This is a possible distinction requiring a full related-work comparison and adequate confirmatory evidence; it is not an established novelty claim or a prediction of WWW acceptance. No inference expenditure is needed to narrow the claim or perform that comparison.
