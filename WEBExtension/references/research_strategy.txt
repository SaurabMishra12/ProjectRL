Research strategy for making SparseComm a strong WWW 2027 submission
My recommendation is to rebuild the paper around one scientific question rather than present SparseComm as another communication-efficient multi-agent framework.
When does communication between retrieval-augmented agents actually contribute useful information, and when do changes in routing, message content, or stopping merely reduce communication without improving the final answer?

This is more substantial than asking whether a learned communication graph uses fewer tokens. It connects communication efficiency to a fundamental question in agentic Web information seeking: what information is worth sharing, with whom, and at what cost?
I examined the two manuscripts and three AI-generated assessments. The reviews range from Reject to Borderline, but they largely agree on the scientific weaknesses.

The third figure means the controller selected the same two-edge graph on every evaluated question, not that zero questions benefited.
The most important observation is that the current sparse policy scores 44.51 F1, while a NoComm ensemble scores 46.79 F1 with zero inter-agent communication. The sparse condition is therefore dominated on the paper's reported communication/F1 axes. Broadcast scores 48.36 F1 but at considerably higher recorded communication.
This does not prove that communication is generally harmful. It reveals a more interesting, unanswered question: why does exchanging additional agent messages fail to improve certain answers, even when retrieval evidence is available?
Your original ACL manuscript provides useful context but cannot directly resolve this question. It evaluates mathematical reasoning and closed-book QA, includes a broader 15-topology experiment on GSM8K, and uses a different communication implementation. The WWW manuscript instead studies candidate-based retrieval with sentence-prefix message selection. These results should be linked as motivation, not treated as interchangeable evidence.
That distinction is central to building a credible finding paper.

The gap I would pursue
Existing literature investigates graph optimization, message pruning, error propagation, and efficiency. The remaining opportunity is a more specific, experimentally testable question:
Does communication become useful when one agent possesses relevant evidence that another agent lacks—and how much of that usefulness depends on where and what we transmit?
This is a candidate research gap, not a claim that nobody has previously studied information sharing. Its novelty would come from a controlled evidence-distribution × communication-allocation evaluation, explicitly tied to multi-hop Web information seeking.
A meaningful contribution would establish three things:
1. Whether the value of inter-agent communication changes between redundant-evidence and complementary-evidence settings.
2. Whether routing and content decisions change delivery of the supporting facts needed for multi-hop answers, even under a fixed communication budget.
3. Whether the savings reported by conventional sparse-agent evaluations remain worthwhile after including answer quality, inference calls, and full token costs.
The literature makes the motivation credible, but new experiments must establish these findings. They cannot be claimed from the existing W1–W4 results.

This requires a change to the existing Web harness, because the current three agents receive the same retrieved passages.
I would use a prespecified HotpotQA sample, randomly assign the document partitions without looking at gold answers, and evaluate whether communication delivers supporting facts previously unavailable to the recipient. Gold supporting-fact labels can be used afterward to characterize evidence coverage, but not to choose the primary routing or partition.
The primary quantity should be the improvement over a NoComm ensemble within each evidence regime, followed by an interaction comparing those improvements. That isolates a much more useful question than whether one graph has a higher average F1.
The strongest result could be a positive effect, a negative effect, or a carefully measured boundary: for example, communication may be helpful only when missing facts are actually transferred, and harmful when it merely repeats misleading claims. These are hypotheses, not existing findings.
Experiment P2: A properly controlled routing intervention
The third review recommends comparing two-edge graphs with frozen messages. That is affordable, but those graph switches can also alter which roles receive updates.
A better-balanced experiment uses opposite directed three-agent cycles:
Clockwise delivery
0 → 1 → 2 → 0


Counterclockwise delivery
0 → 2 → 1 → 0



For each question, both conditions transmit exactly the same initial source messages. Every agent sends once, receives once, and performs one receiver update. Role instructions and condition-neutral prompts are kept fixed.
This balances sender participation, recipient invocation, and payload volume. It measures a controlled recipient-allocation effect, not learned adaptive routing. A separate second-round test would be needed to study propagation of newly revised messages.
If P1 is unaffordable before the deadline, P2 plus an actual-token audit is the minimum worthwhile experimental improvement. I would add P3 only after P2 is stable.

One important repository issue
I also checked the public ProjectRL repository, including its experimental manifest, legacy audit and publicly accessible result records.
The public experiment manifest identifies an October 7 evaluation suite. However, rows visible in the W4 result file referenced by that manifest contain unequal recorded communication counts across routing conditions. Your manuscript specifically describes a later corrected W4 experiment with matched proxy volume.
Those may be different experiment versions. The corrected W4 file path, run identifier and checksums need to be explicitly documented before submission. This is a reproducibility problem worth resolving immediately, not evidence by itself that the corrected manuscript numbers are wrong.
The same priority applies to restoring the controller checkpoint, identifying the exact execution source revision, and making sure cached responses cannot be reused across mismatched prompts or models. I have inspected accessible repository materials but have not rerun the experiments or independently verified every code path.
5. How I would tell the final story
I would organize the paper around the following progression:

Observation: Sparse communication reduces transmitted text, but the sparse policy is not necessarily a better cost–quality operating point than NoComm.



Hypothesis: Communication's value depends on whether missing, useful evidence can reach agents that lack it—not simply on edge count or message length.



Intervention: Randomize evidence distribution, control message delivery and budget, and compare communication against no communication on the same initial states.



Finding: Report where communication improves, harms, or leaves answers unchanged, tied to delivered evidence and measured inference cost.



Contribution: Give researchers a reusable way to determine whether agent-to-agent communication buys useful information rather than just incurs overhead.



A possible title, only if the evidence-intervention experiment supports that framing, is:
When Does Agent Communication Help Multi-Hop Web QA? Evidence Availability and Budget-Matched Message Delivery.
If that experiment cannot be completed, retain Routing or Rationing? and position the contribution as a controlled communication-evaluation study.
6. Submission readiness
The official WWW 2027 Research Track call gives an October 18, 2026 abstract deadline and an October 25, 2026 full-paper deadline, both Anywhere on Earth. It requires the first eight pages of a long paper to be self-contained, with at most 12 total pages including references and appendix. The Evaluation, Human Computation, and Resources track is a defensible choice.

The most important near-term decision is to freeze a feasible primary experiment now. With the deadline this close, a rigorously controlled result is more valuable than another loosely controlled table.
