Abstract
Multi-agent language-model systems pay for
coordination in tokens: in fully connected debate each agent broadcasts its full reasoning
trace to every peer every round, which costs
2914 transmitted inter-agent tokens per question on closed-book HotpotQA in our setup.
Cutting that cost changes two things at once,
which channels carry a message and how much
each carries, and the resulting saving is reported for the pipeline as a whole. We show
that the experiment this factorization most naturally suggests cannot separate the two, and run
one that can.

Introduction

051

Multi-agent language-model (LLM) systems coordinate by exchanging text. Debate and collaboration protocols improve mathematical and factual
reasoning (Du et al., 2024; Liang et al., 2024), software engineering (Hong et al., 2024; Qian et al.,
2024), and open-ended collaboration (Wu et al.,
2024; Li et al., 2023; Zhuge et al., 2024). They
also pay for it: in the fully connected protocol every agent sends its full reasoning trace to every
other agent at every round, so message cost grows
with N 2 and with depth. On closed-book HotpotQA in our setup that comes to 2914 transmitted
inter-agent tokens per question.
Two decisions govern that cost, and they are
usually made together. Routing determines which
directed channels participate. Rationing determines how much reasoning content a participating
channel carries. Communication-efficient methods prune agents or messages (Zhang et al., 2025a;
Wang et al., 2025); topology-learning methods optimize the graph (Zhang et al., 2025b; Shen et al.,
2025; Li et al., 2026). Either way the reported gain
is a property of the whole policy. When such a
paper attributes its saving to a better topology, the
attribution is not tested by the experiment it reports,
because in an LLM system a graph edit is also a
token edit: dropping a channel removes a variablelength natural-language message whose size the
experimenter never chose.
This paper is about that attribution problem, and
S PARSE C OMM is the instrument we use to study
it. S PARSE C OMM is a shared actor–critic whose
state summarizes collective disagreement, reasoning overlap, answer diversity, confidence, consen-

What remains open is which component delivers
the saving. Pruning an agent removes both its edges
and its messages, so the reduction is reported for
the pipeline; nothing in these designs distinguishes
a graph effect from a message-length effect, and
our Proposition 2 says nothing in a factorial version
of them would either.
Learning the graph. A third line optimizes the
communication graph itself (Zhang et al., 2025b;
Li et al., 2026; Zhuge et al., 2024; Shen et al.,
2025), extending an older multi-agent RL literature
on gated and targeted communication (Sukhbaatar
et al., 2016; Foerster et al., 2016; Das et al., 2019;
Singh et al., 2019; Jiang and Lu, 2018). The MARL
work is the closest formal antecedent: it also learns
continuous communication weights. But in MARL
a message is a fixed-width vector, so a gate value
changes what is sent without changing how much
is sent, and a topology contrast is automatically
volume-controlled. LLM agents break that property. Messages are natural-language objects of variable length, so a gate edit changes the transmitted
token count by an amount nobody chose. §6.2
measures how large that amount is.

3

What we add. Prior work optimizes communication structure for downstream performance
and reports the resulting savings. We ask what
a component-wise intervention on such a structure
can identify, answer that question in the negative
for the design the factorization most naturally suggests (§5.4), and then run the design that comes
closest to answering it (§6.3). The closest competing approaches, AgentPrune (Zhang et al., 2025a)
and AgentDropout (Wang et al., 2025), share our
goal of cheap communication but not our question; G-Designer (Zhang et al., 2025b) and ARGDesigner (Li et al., 2026) share our interest in the
graph but read their graph comparisons as topology
effects. Table 1 positions S PARSE C OMM by reported mechanism. We have not run these systems,
so it is not a performance comparison and we make
no claim about relative cost or accuracy.

100% training accuracy there while held-out accuracy stays near 32% (Table 13), so we do not read
its routing statistics as evidence about multi-hop
reasoning, and no mechanism claim in this paper
rests on HotpotQA.

over the complete graph every round and stops
early on unanimity (Du et al., 2024; Liang et al.,
2024). All methods share the backbone, the cap
of three post-initial rounds, the aggregator and the
calibration.
We did not re-run the systems of Table 1. Our
comparison is against fully connected debate and a
zero-communication ensemble, we make no claim
of state-of-the-art communication efficiency, and
we do not know where S PARSE C OMM sits relative
to existing pruning methods on the cost–accuracy
plane. A frozen-controller transfer check on a second backbone is in Appendix J. Token counts are
not compute counts: the retention filter runs a 22Mparameter bi-encoder over every sentence of every trace, whose cost we did not measure, so the
reported savings are savings in inter-agent LLM
tokens and nothing more.

The exact-volume comparison

Proposition 3 asks for equal transmitted mass. We
implement it directly: every arm receives the same
per-question transmitted budget, spread over whatever channels its support opens. A single routine truncates messages at their allocations and
reallocates any unused quota until the tokenizercounted sum is exactly V̄ . Round-0 traces and the
set of communication-eligible questions are shared

semantic sentence selection inside the transmission
path, so the message rule is invariant as Proposition 3 requires. We evaluate V̄ ∈ {50, 100}.

Conclusion

840
841

Routing and rationing are separate decisions, and
the experiments the field runs do not separate them.
A PPO-trained controller over continuous edge intensities, with semantic retention and learned stopping, cuts inter-agent traffic by 50–90% on three
reasoning benchmarks and is non-inferior to fully
connected debate at a 5-point margin. Attributing that saving to a component is harder than it
looks: switching the routing factor alone moves
transmitted volume by a factor of 3.4 while switching the message rule alone moves it by 1.06, so the
unconstrained 2 × 2 estimates a total effect with
volume as mediator. Fixing transmitted volume
and varying the support separates the parts. Doing
so on GSM8K leaves the complete graph, a star
and five random two-edge supports in a band spanning 75.33–86.00% around a zero-communication
reference of 78.67%, while the learned support
reaches 95.33 and 92.67. Neither per-channel message length nor channel count explains the gap; a
graph-isomorphic broadcast rooted at a different
agent loses fourteen to fifteen points. In this setting,
under a scarce budget, what a routing policy contributes is the selection of an information source,
which is a different object from the bandwidth allocation the literature has largely optimized.

score. We therefore call S a halting score rather

score is enabled only after the first communication
round. Each controller trains for five outer passes;
