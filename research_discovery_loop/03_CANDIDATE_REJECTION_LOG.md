# Candidate generation and rejection log

Nine distinct internal questions were considered. Zero survived the final hard gates. Generation is exploratory investigator reasoning, not evidence of novelty. Four reviewer perspectives are separately articulated in report 10; they are simulations by the same investigator and do not constitute independent peer review. The three detailed rejected design records in 05 preserve the strongest attempts; they are not an endorsed shortlist.

## K1 — Do recipient activations, rather than edge routing, explain reported sparse-agent savings?

Why it matters: Operators must know whether to optimize participation or communication.

Current knowledge: L1/L2/L4/L5 already distinguish participation, edges and execution costs. Unresolved: The exact controlled direct effect of evidence routing at fixed revision schedule remains unmeasured here.

Repository evidence: E5 has 912 recipient-count mismatches; P seed 19/31 each activate one receiver on all 300 questions; W4 is inconclusive.

Missing evidence: A multi-system effect distinct from known node pruning; nondegenerate randomization support and provider-level cost logs.

Decisive intervention considered: Freeze all initial states, activate all receivers under both peer and own-state updates, randomize source assignments and match token ceilings; use N>=4 where in-star support needs rewiring.

Technical feasibility: Offline instrumentation is feasible; a scientifically qualified population and full study are not established. No inference is authorized.

If positive: A transportable decomposition could tell designers which execution component to optimize.

If negative: An adequately precise zero routing effect would bound value for the registered systems, not establish universal absence.

Strongest reviewer objection and final hard-gate reason: **No substantive novelty established beyond existing component diagnosis; dominant N=3 in-star has no alternative graph conditional on its active recipient and edge count.**

Closest work: L1, L2, L4, L5; see primary-source method comparisons in 02. Verdict: **REJECT for this submission direction.**

## K2 — Does source/role assignment explain apparent communication-topology advantages?

Why it matters: Web agents receive unequal evidence and expertise, so arbitrary seat choices can distort comparisons.

Current knowledge: L3 explicitly routes agent-to-role permutations; L1 includes role removals. Unresolved: Role-by-evidence interactions under source-grounded correctness are not identified by our CW/CCW contrast.

Repository evidence: Legacy permutations vary absolute scores; E2 CW/CCW uses the same cycle motif and asymmetric labeled sources.

Missing evidence: Crossed role/evidence permutations with order-invariant readout and multiple frozen source regimes.

Decisive intervention considered: Randomize role, source bundle and graph labels independently before outputs; pair all configurations by question.

Technical feasibility: Offline instrumentation is feasible; a scientifically qualified population and full study are not established. No inference is authorized.

If positive: A general interaction could guide placement of specialized Web agents.

If negative: A narrow null would show label robustness for the chosen prompts only.

Strongest reviewer objection and final hard-gate reason: **A Web benchmark extension of already explicit positional-routing work; legacy evidence lacks clean provenance and independent replication.**

Closest work: L3, L1, L5; see primary-source method comparisons in 02. Verdict: **REJECT for this submission direction.**

## K3 — When does repeated upstream Web evidence become false independent corroboration during agent communication?

Why it matters: Syndication and copying can amplify a single erroneous source into apparent agreement.

Current knowledge: L7 directly manipulates shared-source descendants; L11 studies evidence imbalance and misinformation. Unresolved: A causal contrast on genuinely traced Web copying, rather than synthetic copies, would be more specific.

Repository evidence: Saved facts have titles and sentence IDs, but these do not certify upstream independence; repeated proposals in E8 are only a diagnostic.

Missing evidence: A licensed corpus with validated derivation lineages, matched independent alternatives and blinded factuality labels.

Decisive intervention considered: Randomize lineage duplication versus independent corroboration at fixed semantic evidence, length, agents and readout; distinguish metadata disclosure from actual dependence.

Technical feasibility: Offline instrumentation is feasible; a scientifically qualified population and full study are not established. No inference is authorized.

If positive: A new real-source mechanism could improve provenance-sensitive Web evidence handling.

If negative: A precise null would be informative only if lineage and intervention exposure were validated.

Strongest reviewer objection and final hard-gate reason: **The proposed synthetic mechanism is directly studied by L7; a real-Web version lacks a suitable identified evaluation population here.**

Closest work: L7, L11, L10; see primary-source method comparisons in 02. Verdict: **REJECT for this submission direction.**

## K4 — Are failures limited mainly by missing retrieval evidence or by failure to use available evidence?

Why it matters: Retrieval and communication require different remedies in multi-hop search.

Current knowledge: L10 formalizes sufficiency; L6 directly tests split-evidence exchange; L11 studies document access. Unresolved: A quantified randomized retrieval-by-communication interaction is absent from current source-qualified data.

Repository evidence: Only 76/300 old pools contain all annotated support; E6 has 8 lexical-answer-bearing pools and no independent necessity qualification.

Missing evidence: Independent source-sufficiency annotations and an intervention that varies accessibility without changing difficulty or answer leakage.

Decisive intervention considered: Randomize access to validated complementary sources and peer versus private revision, scoring supported answers and valid abstention separately.

Technical feasibility: Offline instrumentation is feasible; a scientifically qualified population and full study are not established. No inference is authorized.

If positive: Could locate a causal bottleneck in a specified retrieval pipeline.

If negative: A null remains ambiguous without proving that access changes meaningful evidence availability.

Strongest reviewer objection and final hard-gate reason: **The broad retrieval/utilization question is established; treating missing support as unanswerability invalidates the proposed shortcut.**

Closest work: L10, L6, L11; see primary-source method comparisons in 02. Verdict: **REJECT for this submission direction.**

## K5 — Does source sufficiency determine how aggregation changes the measured value and harm of communication?

Why it matters: A readout that raises answerable EM may also select unsupported answers.

Current knowledge: L8 separates proposal/readout; L10 and L12 study sufficiency and abstention. Unresolved: A balanced interaction on semantically qualified paired Web contexts is unmeasured.

Repository evidence: Frozen P gain changes from 8 pp to 1 pp under aware voting, but remains 7.667 pp under strict majority; external arithmetic has no semantic-abstention intervention.

Missing evidence: Qualified labels, matched private revisions, immutable backbones and variance small enough for the independent population.

Decisive intervention considered: On paired sufficient/insufficient contexts, compare peer versus own-state revision and apply frozen readouts offline; primary question-clustered utility interaction.

Technical feasibility: Offline instrumentation is feasible; a scientifically qualified population and full study are not established. No inference is authorized.

If positive: Could identify a source-conditioned boundary for interpreting collaboration benefits.

If negative: A tight null would bound the interaction for those contexts; weak abstention prevalence makes a null uninformative.

Strongest reviewer objection and final hard-gate reason: **No substantive mechanism beyond combined existing questions established; current external traces are ineligible, labels pending and high-variance power exceeds the available pool.**

Closest work: L8, L10, L12; see primary-source method comparisons in 02. Verdict: **REJECT for this submission direction.**

## K6 — Do sparse routing policies transfer across changes in Web evidence availability and conflict?

Why it matters: Policies trained on one search regime may fail under retrieval shifts.

Current knowledge: L5 tests domain transfer and L2 transfers across graph generators. Unresolved: Evidence-regime transfer is a specific untested axis, but needs a mechanism beyond ordinary distribution shift.

Repository evidence: E5 policies differ greatly across seeds and collapse to simple activity patterns; no qualified transfer trial exists.

Missing evidence: Multiple frozen policies, independent train/test source regimes, common readout and meaningful interaction effect.

Decisive intervention considered: Evaluate frozen policies on outcome-independent source corruption/access shifts against validation-selected static and random activity-matched controls.

Technical feasibility: Offline instrumentation is feasible; a scientifically qualified population and full study are not established. No inference is authorized.

If positive: Might reveal a reusable failure mode in policy deployment.

If negative: Failure alone could reflect local training bugs, poor policies or generic shift; success need not explain a mechanism.

Closest work: L2, L5, L3; see primary-source method comparisons in 02. Verdict: **REJECT for this submission direction.**

## K7 — Does answer-conditioned message compression amplify an agent’s incorrect initial answer at fixed bandwidth?

Why it matters: Short handoffs may preserve persuasive conclusions while deleting disconfirming evidence.

Current knowledge: L6 compares message content and grounding; L8 intervenes on verification content. Unresolved: The exact directional harm of a sender-answer-conditioned selector has not been isolated here.

Repository evidence: Legacy compressor scores sentences against the sender’s own extracted answer; E3-v2 is fixed-length evidence position, not this selector.

Missing evidence: A validated non-gold selector intervention with neutral and evidence-preserving controls, semantic adequacy labels and replicated harm.

Decisive intervention considered: Freeze sender texts; randomize answer-conditioned versus query-conditioned versus randomized exact-length selections; all receivers revise with fixed roles/readout.

Technical feasibility: Offline instrumentation is feasible; a scientifically qualified population and full study are not established. No inference is authorized.

If positive: Might quantify a specific harmful selection mechanism.

If negative: A null could mean selector equivalence or weak manipulation, rather than absence of anchoring.

Strongest reviewer objection and final hard-gate reason: **At present it diagnoses a local heuristic; no established distinction from content/grounding interventions and no qualified manipulation population.**

Closest work: L6, L8, L11; see primary-source method comparisons in 02. Verdict: **REJECT for this submission direction.**

## K8 — Can consensus stopping avoid losing already-correct answers under dependent communication?

Why it matters: Premature convergence can turn agreement into a misleading stopping signal.

Current knowledge: L14 studies consensus calibration failure; L8 measures damage; L3 studies influence and error cascades. Unresolved: A correctness-sensitive sequential guarantee under realistic dependence is absent, not supplied by ordinary SPRT thresholds.

Repository evidence: E4 has 6 wrong unanimous stops, no observed correction on continuation, identical final F1 and only hypothetical savings.

Missing evidence: Adequately many fresh late-correction cases and a calibrated stopping policy selected without test outcomes.

Decisive intervention considered: Pre-register stopping rule; retain full trajectories in evaluation only; compare to fixed-depth and matched private-compute controls across source regimes.

Technical feasibility: Offline instrumentation is feasible; a scientifically qualified population and full study are not established. No inference is authorized.

If positive: Could support a dependable deployment criterion if calibration transfers.

If negative: No late corrections in a small sample cannot certify safety; a sufficiently precise harm bound would be required.

Strongest reviewer objection and final hard-gate reason: **Stopping and its calibration limits are already studied; repository trajectories do not identify a new dependence mechanism or validate a correctness guarantee.**

Closest work: L14, L8, L3; see primary-source method comparisons in 02. Verdict: **REJECT for this submission direction.**

## K9 — Do provider failures and refusals alter the effective evidence graph and bias efficiency comparisons?

Why it matters: Retries and unavailable agents change real deployment costs and accessible evidence.

Current knowledge: L1 already disables workers and records retries/fallbacks; abstention differs from transport failures. Unresolved: Provider-specific missingness effects require a defined target population and failure mechanism.

Repository evidence: Historical E5 has uncertain attempts and incomplete usage; E6 has 0 failures in 144 calls, unsuitable for estimating failure impact.

Missing evidence: A representative failure distribution, declared failover semantics, and complete timestamped provider accounting.

Decisive intervention considered: Randomize worker availability/failure injection before outcomes, freeze failover and include all assigned questions in intention-to-treat analysis.

Technical feasibility: Offline instrumentation is feasible; a scientifically qualified population and full study are not established. No inference is authorized.

If positive: Could improve resilient execution if realistic Web consequences differ from existing worker-failure studies.

If negative: An artificial failure null generalizes only to its injection law and could be operationally irrelevant.

Strongest reviewer objection and final hard-gate reason: **Existing worker-failure benchmark overlap and no representative local population; ledger repair alone is engineering.**

Closest work: L1, L12, L14; see primary-source method comparisons in 02. Verdict: **REJECT for this submission direction.**
