For the K5 paired-context interaction, each question outcome lies within[-2,+2], not necessarily[-1,+1]. A conservative range-based SD ceiling 2 is a loose bound, not a realistic variance estimate. Planning normal approximation is N=ceil[(z_(1-alpha/2)+z_power)^2*sigma^2/delta^2]. Alpha=.025 per backbone controls a two-model family at 0.05. Using a separate primary hypothesis for each of nine candidates would need a new multiplicity plan; these are designs being rejected, not nine tests being run.

## Reproducible bounded sensitivity

The power script generates a source-realizable J in{-1,0,+1}, with probabilities[(s²-d)/2,1-s²,(s²+d)/2], mean d and actual variance s²-d². The multinomial simulation reports a studentized two-sided rejection rate and Monte Carlo error. J=+1 can be realized by a sufficient-context peer set[abstain,abstain,correct] versus private[correct,correct,correct], and an insufficient-context peer set[abstain,abstain,abstain] versus private[abstain,abstain,wrong]; swap peer/private for J=-1, identical arms for 0. This is a constructed sensitivity law, not a forecast. SD 2 is not simulated under this law. At N=238 with nominal SD parameter 0.25 the constructed-law rejection rate is 87.24% (Monte Carlo SE 0.236 pp); at N=951 and 0.5 it is 80.54% (SE 0.280 pp). These are simulated law-specific rates, not population power guarantees.

## Adverse-outcome interpretation

A precise adverse result can be valuable if the target population and manipulation are valid and the question is distinct from prior work. Wide nulls, invalid source labels, zero abstention exposure, local bugs and unvalidated natural-lineage controls do not deliver such a result. No amount of token budgeting repairs a fatal novelty or identification flaw.
